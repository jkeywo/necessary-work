# The Necessary Work — Development Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap develops *The Necessary Work* by proving the core incremental economy before adding the more politically and educationally demanding 2020 scenario.

> Do not use additional content, educational ambition, or technical elegance to explain away an unsatisfying core loop.

## Stage 1 — First Prototype

### Primary design test

Determine whether the core economy is satisfying over a 20–30 minute run.

The prototype succeeds when players can:

- understand emissions, Finance, Mandate, projects, icons, and programme slots;
- distinguish direct emissions reductions from enabling investments;
- create momentum through icon accumulation and explicit breakpoints;
- make meaningful choices between immediate reduction and future capacity;
- reach global gross emissions zero through more than one plausible build order;
- remain engaged without player-facing speed controls.

### Scenario

- Counterfactual 1990.
- CFA administrative headquarters in Europe.
- Europe receives no inherent technical, economic, or efficiency bonus.
- The first programme can begin immediately.
- No loss state; the run is structurally recoverable.
- Victory: global gross emissions reach zero and ongoing Finance and Mandate deltas are non-negative.
- No carbon removal.
- Sector baselines do not change without projects.

### Included systems

- Three continental regions.
- Three emissions sectors per continent.
- Finance and Mandate.
- Four continental icon families.
- Approximately 12 projects.
- One global programme slot initially.
- A second global slot unlocked early through a continental Institutions milestone.
- A third global slot unlocked around the midpoint through a later continental Institutions milestone.
- Planning queue.
- Project prerequisites, scaling, and breakpoints.
- Projected outcome previews.
- Layered calculation explanations.
- Pause with full planning while paused.
- Persistent notifications.
- Simple deterministic beneficial opportunities.
- Autosave, manual save/load, deterministic action log, and replay validation.
- Local completion records and provisional benchmark brackets.
- End-screen causal recap.

### Deliberately rough

- Placeholder art.
- Functional rather than polished interface.
- Provisional values.
- Limited project prose.
- Simplified geography and units.
- Limited event variety.

### Must be reliable

- Core simulation.
- Queue behaviour.
- Project previews.
- Calculation traces.
- Deterministic randomness.
- Save and replay validation.
- Completion timing and end-state validation.

### Exit decision

Proceed only if structured playtesting shows that:

- the loop is understandable;
- it supports multiple plausible build orders;
- enabling investment feels strategic rather than compulsory;
- normal-speed play remains engaging for 20–30 minutes;
- waiting creates planning or anticipation rather than inactivity.

If players understand the economy but find it repetitive, passive, or obvious, revise the economy before expanding the game.

## Stage 2 — Vertical Slice

### Goal

Demonstrate intended production quality and confirm that the prototype economy survives richer content and presentation.

### Additions

- Representative final-quality visual direction.
- Stronger sound and completion feedback.
- Refined project cards and side panels.
- Polished onboarding.
- Better project context and source presentation.
- Milestone activation profiles.
- More robust accessibility settings.
- Expanded playtest instrumentation.
- Empirical benchmark data collection.
- One carefully authored example of each planned project behaviour using the existing effect vocabulary.

### Gate

Presentation must improve comprehension and satisfaction without concealing a weak or dominant economy.

## Stage 3 — Alpha 1: Complete 1990 Mode

### Goal

Produce a replayable, coherent version of the 1990 scenario.

### Additions

- Full 1990 project catalogue.
- Stable balancing and multiple viable strategies.
- Refined regional starting conditions.
- Improved opportunity variety.
- Complete project evidence panels.
- Versioned local records.
- Benchmark brackets increasingly derived from validated developer, bot, and playtest runs.
- Archived records for prior ruleset versions.
- Wider native Windows testing and supported WASM build.

### Exclusions retained

- No loss state.
- No climate crises.
- No Protection or Legitimacy.
- No local-consent simulation.
- No carbon removal.
- No dynamic sector baselines.
- No online leaderboard.
- No offline progress.

## Stage 4 — 2020 Mode

Introduce the consequences of delayed action and the political and protective systems absent from 1990:

- genuine loss condition;
- Protection;
- Legitimacy;
- climate crises;
- sector baseline growth or decline;
- stronger path dependence;
- distributional consequences;
- local legitimacy and delivery constraints;
- adaptation;
- carbon removal only after its role, limits, and risks are modelled clearly;
- historically grounded obstruction and institutional constraints.

This requires a separate design pass, not merely more content for the 1990 economy.

## Stage 5 — Release

- Clear distinction between counterfactual 1990 and constrained 2020.
- Educational claims reviewed against current evidence.
- Regional framing reviewed for hierarchy and deficit narratives.
- Stable ruleset versioning.
- Robust save migration policy or explicit compatibility boundaries.
- Production-quality interface, audio, feedback, onboarding, and accessibility.
- Privacy and service design completed before any online scoring system.
