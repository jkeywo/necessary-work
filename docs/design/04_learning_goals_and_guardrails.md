# The Necessary Work — Learning Goals and Guardrails

## Status

The first prototype primarily tests whether the economy is satisfying. It does not need to prove the complete educational thesis, but it must avoid teaching harmful or misleading causal lessons.

## Intended learning direction

The eventual game should help players understand that:

1. Emissions reduction is a systems problem, not a list of isolated technologies.
2. Immediate deployment and long-term capacity building are both necessary.
3. Infrastructure, institutions, labour, and knowledge determine deployment speed.
4. Electricity decarbonisation enables but does not complete other transitions.
5. Timing matters because emissions accumulate and delay constrains later choices.
6. Vulnerability is shaped by poverty, power, infrastructure, ecology, and institutions rather than geography alone.
7. A just transition has independent social value.
8. Historical responsibility and unequal capacity affect fairness.
9. Adaptation cannot substitute for mitigation.
10. Carbon removal cannot justify delaying gross reductions.

## Prototype learning goals

The 1990 prototype should support four modest insights:

- direct-reduction projects can be immediately valuable;
- enabling projects can strengthen later deployment;
- sectors and continents have different installed-system bottlenecks;
- coordination capacity can expand concurrent programmes.

These should emerge through play rather than compulsory instruction.

## Prototype non-goals

- Complete climate-political history since 1990.
- Precise regional responsibility.
- Precise technology costs or deployment potential.
- National or local political consent.
- Adaptation and damages.
- Carbon removal.
- Detailed macroeconomics.
- Individual lifestyle accounting.

## Guardrails

### Regional framing

Do not imply that poorer regions are the main obstacle, Europe or North America are inherently more capable, the Majority World is homogeneous, regional differences are permanent traits, development opposes decarbonisation, or energy access should be frozen.

Differences must be authored scenario conditions involving installed systems, finance, infrastructure, workforce, institutions, historical emissions, and bottlenecks.

“Majority World” is provisional and must be reviewed before public release.

### European headquarters

Europe hosts the initial CFA secretariat for contingent historical and treaty reasons. It grants no innate regional bonus and does not imply ownership or superiority. Its only mechanical effect is that the first programme can begin there.

### CFA authority

The CFA operates through ratified mandates, coordinates and finances programmes, relies on member institutions and local implementation, cannot simply bypass democratic authority, and has responsibilities to representation, equity, transparency, and accountability.

Prototype Mandate is global treaty coordination authority, not local consent.

### Technology and policy

Do not imply that one technology is universal, effects are precisely known, deployment is frictionless once funded, social programmes matter only instrumentally, electrification removes the need for planning or demand reduction, or negative emissions can rapidly undo accumulated warming.

### Quantification

Values are illustrative, rounded, balance-driven, and not forecasts. Every project panel should separate:

1. Evidence.
2. Game abstraction.
3. Scenario mechanics.

### Counterfactual framing

The 1990 scenario is not a claim that rapid transition would have been simple or uncontested. It omits historical obstruction, crises, and political conflict to isolate the economy. Public-facing material must state this.

The lack of a loss state means mechanical recoverability, not that real delay was harmless. Completion time is a replay metric, not a moral grade.

## Scoring guardrails

The end screen may compare completion time with benchmark brackets. It must not moralise, claim a false global percentile, hide handcrafted early brackets, compare incompatible versions, or punish reading through a separate score.

Prefer “reference target,” “typical reference range,” and “based on developer and test runs.” Avoid moral medals, letter grades, or “you saved the planet.”

## Review process

Before vertical slice:

- review regional descriptions for deficit framing;
- verify each project claim against strong sources;
- identify omitted constraints;
- ensure icons represent installed capacity rather than cultural superiority;
- test explanations with non-specialists.

Before public Alpha:

- climate mitigation review;
- climate justice/development review;
- educational-game or science-communication review;
- revise misleading inferences found in playtests;
- publish known abstractions and disagreements.

## Playtest questions

- What did the icons represent?
- Why did projects improve later?
- What did Mandate represent?
- Why could the CFA act in one place before another?
- Did any continent seem inherently deficient or superior?
- Did the game imply technology alone solved the problem?
- Did the score feel moralising?
- What real-world constraints seemed absent?
