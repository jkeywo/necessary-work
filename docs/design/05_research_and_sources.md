# The Necessary Work — Research and Sources Plan

## Purpose

Evidence supports the game but does not directly determine prototype balance values. Every project should connect sources to a specific mechanic and separately document the abstraction used for play.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Major scientific assessments and synthesis reports.
2. Official datasets and statistical agencies.
3. Peer-reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
4. Specialist institutional reports with transparent methods.
5. Individual peer-reviewed studies.
6. Design inspirations and explanatory journalism, clearly labelled.

Do not base contested mechanics on a single advocacy, industry, or popular source.

## Core climate science

### IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report

Use for cumulative emissions, urgency, mitigation pathways, adaptation/mitigation interactions, equity, uncertainty, and delayed-action limits.

https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/

### IPCC Working Group III

Use for sector options, enabling conditions, finance, institutions, demand-side mitigation, feasibility, barriers, and just-transition implications.

https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/

### IPCC Working Group II

Primarily for 2020 mode: vulnerability, adaptation, regional risk, equity, limits to adaptation, and climate-resilient development.

https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/

## Transition pathways

### International Energy Agency — Net Zero Roadmap, 2023 update

Use for electricity sequencing, electrification, efficiency, demand changes, technology maturity, infrastructure dependencies, energy access, and limits on future technologies.

https://www.iea.org/reports/net-zero-roadmap-a-global-pathway-to-keep-the-15-c-goal-in-reach

### UNEP Emissions Gap Report

Use for the gap between commitments and action, pathway comparison, and contemporary context for 2020 mode. Recheck the newest edition before public milestones.

https://www.unep.org/resources/emissions-gap-report

## Emissions data

### Climate Watch / World Resources Institute

Use for emissions by sector, historical emissions, and country/regional data.

https://www.climatewatchdata.org/

### Our World in Data

Use as an accessible secondary presentation layer while retaining original dataset citations.

https://ourworldindata.org/co2-and-greenhouse-gas-emissions

## Sector evidence

### Power

Wind, solar, nuclear, grids, storage, demand response, transmission, fossil retirement, market reform, workforce, and supply chains.

Likely sources: IEA, IRENA, IPCC WGIII, national laboratories, and peer-reviewed grid-integration reviews.

### Transport and Buildings

Public transport, active travel, compact development, EVs, freight, heat pumps, retrofit, standards, material demand, behaviour, and avoided demand.

Do not reduce the sector to private EVs and appliance replacement.

### Industry and Land

Material efficiency, circularity, steel, cement, industrial heat, methane, fertiliser, food systems, ecosystem protection, restoration, and land conversion.

Project context must explain that these are grouped for prototype simplicity.

## Capacity-icon research

### Knowledge

R&D, demonstration, standards, knowledge transfer, and institutional learning.

### Infrastructure

Grids, transport networks, manufacturing, ports, supply chains, and building stock.

### Workforce

Training time, labour shortages, occupational transition, organised labour, employment, and just-transition policy.

### Institutions

Planning capacity, administration, permitting, procurement, regulation, coordination, and public finance institutions.

Icons must not be justified through broad claims about culture or governance quality.

## Equity, development, and historical responsibility

Required before 2020 mode:

- cumulative and consumption-based emissions;
- differentiated responsibility;
- development needs and energy access;
- climate finance, loss and damage, and technology transfer;
- debt and fiscal capacity;
- colonial and extractive histories;
- just transition, procedural justice, and distributional effects.

Potential sources: IPCC, UNFCCC, UNEP, UNDP, ILO, critically used World Bank datasets, and peer-reviewed climate-justice scholarship.

Include sources that challenge preferred interventions and expose trade-offs.

## Carbon removal

Excluded from the prototype. Before addition, cover separate accounting for reduction/removal, permanence, reversal, land competition, energy, monitoring, governance, mitigation deterrence, deployment rates, and differences between biological and technological removal.

## Educational and game-design research

Questions:

- What do players infer from resource-conversion games?
- When do efficiency scores hide value judgements?
- How should simulations communicate uncertainty?
- How can systemic causation be shown without false precision?
- Does competitive timing discourage reading?
- How should counterfactual climate scenarios communicate omitted conflict?
- What debriefing improves transfer to real-world understanding?

Relevant fields: serious games, simulation learning, systems-thinking education, climate communication, procedural rhetoric, debriefing, and uncertainty visualisation.

Design inspirations such as *Daybreak* are inspirations, not evidence.

## Per-project evidence record

Each project should record:

- identifier and real-world intervention;
- sector and scope;
- strongest supporting sources;
- important limiting or contrary sources;
- evidence strength;
- enabling conditions;
- deployment constraints;
- co-benefits, harms, and equity issues;
- what the game models and omits;
- reason for prototype values;
- review date and reviewer.

## In-game citation structure

- What it is
- Why it matters
- What it depends on
- Limits and trade-offs
- In this scenario
- Sources

## Maintenance

Record access/review dates; recheck changing data before milestones; separate source revisions from balance revisions; version evidence records; do not preserve outdated claims for save compatibility; represent genuine uncertainty rather than inventing precision.
